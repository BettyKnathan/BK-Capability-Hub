Change Adoption

A practical playbook for moving from awareness to sustained behaviour: diagnose barriers, design interventions, support AI adoption and measure whether work actually changed.

Adoption is not attendance. Adoption is behaviour change.
Betty Lens

Change adoption starts with work, not the tool

Successful transformation is not measured by whether people attended training or tried a tool once. It is measured by whether people understand the change, choose to engage, build capability, apply the new behaviour in real work and sustain the behaviour over time.

Business Goal→ Capability Gap→ Adoption Barrier→ Behaviour Change→ Intervention→ Measurement

1. ADKAR: the individual change backbone

Use ADKAR to diagnose where adoption is stuck. Each stage represents an outcome a person needs to move through change successfully.

A

Awareness

Does the person understand why the change is needed?

Design response:
Case for change, leader message, impact story.
D

Desire

Does the person want to participate and support the change?

Design response:
WIIFM, manager support, resistance conversations.
K

Knowledge

Does the person know how to change?

Design response:
Role-based learning, demos, examples, job aids.
A

Ability

Can the person apply the new skill or behaviour in real work?

Design response:
Practice, coaching, workflow support, feedback.
R

Reinforcement

Is the behaviour being sustained?

Design response:
Nudges, recognition, metrics, manager check-ins.

2. AI adoption barriers to diagnose

AI adoption is often blocked by friction, trust, risk, relevance, habit and confidence. The design response should be smaller, clearer and closer to the workflow than a generic training programme.

Friction

“I don't know where to start.”

Support with prompt starters, guided examples and simple first-use pathways.

Trust

“What if it gives the wrong answer?”

Support with validation checklists, quality gates and human-review guidance.

Risk

“Will I expose sensitive data?”

Support with responsible-use reminders, data decision trees and safe-use examples.

Relevance

“How does this help my job?”

Support with role-based use cases and workflow examples.

Habit

“I already have a way of doing this.”

Support with nudges, quick wins and manager reinforcement.

Confidence

“The AI experts know this. I don't.”

Support with safe practice, peer sharing, champions and confidence-building sessions.

4. Transformation considerations

Workflow integration

Move from “learn then work” to “support while working”

Embed prompts, decision trees, checklists, examples and reminders into the moment of need.

Decision scaffolding

Help people decide when and how to use AI

Clarify which tasks are appropriate for AI, what data is safe, what risks to check and who owns the final decision.

Manager reinforcement

Managers convert training into behaviour

Give managers talking points, observation guides, check-in questions and reinforcement prompts.

Responsible adoption

Speed must not outrun judgement

Build responsible-use guardrails, review expectations and escalation points into the adoption journey.

5. Measurement model: beyond usage

Adoption is not only “Are people using it?” A stronger question is: “Has the behaviour changed, and is the change creating value?”

DimensionKey questionExample indicators
AccessCan people use the tool or new way of working?Access readiness, permission issues, enablement gaps.
AwarenessDo people understand why the change matters?Pulse responses, manager feedback, common questions.
First useHave people tried the new behaviour once?First prompt, first workflow use, first completed scenario.
Repeat useAre people coming back?Weekly/monthly repeat behaviour, return users, sustained participation.
Workflow integrationHas work changed?Before/after workflow evidence, use in meetings, drafts, analysis or review points.
Confidence and judgementCan people use the new approach responsibly?Confidence ratings, validation behaviour, escalation decisions.
Value realizationIs the change helping the business?Reduced friction, quality improvement, cycle-time reduction, better decision support.
Useful adoption test: If the tool or new way of working disappeared tomorrow, would people complain because it had become genuinely useful?
