Coaching is not advice-giving

Use this page when the goal is reflection, clarity, ownership and action. The coaching stance is to help the other person think better — not to rush into solving for them.

2. GROW Framework

Expand Questions

Goal

  • What do you want?
  • What does success look like?
  • What outcome would make this conversation useful?

Reality

  • What is happening now?
  • What obstacles exist?
  • What have you already tried?

Options

  • What could you do?
  • What alternatives exist?
  • What would you try if there were fewer constraints?

Way Forward

  • What action will you take?
  • When?
  • What support or accountability will help?

3. OSKAR Framework

Expand Questions
  • Outcome: What would success look like?
  • Scaling: Where are you today on a scale of 1-10?
  • Know-How: What strengths can help?
  • Affirm & Action: What is already working? What small action could move things forward?
  • Review: What progress have you made? What have you learned?

4. The Coaching Habit

Expand Framework
  1. What's on your mind?
  2. And what else?
  3. What's the real challenge here for you?
  4. What do you want?
  5. How can I help?
  6. If you're saying yes to this, what are you saying no to?
  7. What was most useful for you?

5. Coaching Prompt Cards

Use these prompts to prepare for coaching conversations, reflect after conversations, or create question sets for specific leadership situations.

Prepare for a coaching conversation 
Act as an executive coaching preparation partner.

Context:
[Insert coaching context]

Help me prepare by identifying:
1. The possible coaching objective
2. Questions I should ask before giving advice
3. GROW questions I can use
4. OSKAR questions I can use
5. Possible blind spots
6. What I should avoid saying
7. How to help the person leave with ownership and action
Create reflective questions for decision quality 
Act as a reflective executive coach.

Decision I am considering:
[Insert decision]

Create coaching questions that help me separate:
- Facts
- Assumptions
- Risks
- Options
- Trade-offs
- Stakeholder impact
- What should remain human-owned

End with 5 questions that improve judgment before action.
Use The Coaching Habit for a stuck situation 
Act as a coaching conversation designer using The Coaching Habit.

Situation:
[Insert situation]

Create a question sequence using:
1. What's on your mind?
2. And what else?
3. What's the real challenge here for you?
4. What do you want?
5. How can I help?
6. If you're saying yes to this, what are you saying no to?
7. What was most useful for you?

Adapt the questions to sound natural, warm and professional.

Coaching and AI

AI can help draft questions, structure reflection and surface blind spots. The human coach still owns presence, judgment, listening, timing and sensitivity to context.

Design principle: AI can prepare the conversation. The human relationship carries the conversation.
